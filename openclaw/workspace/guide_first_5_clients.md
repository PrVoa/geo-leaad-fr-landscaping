# STRATEGY 1: WARM NETWORK MINING

## The Hidden Gold Mine

You already know people who:
- Work at companies that need your service
- Own businesses
- Know business owners
- Would refer you if asked

## Action Steps

### Step 1: The List
Write down everyone you know. Everyone.
- Former colleagues
- College/school friends
- Family members
- Neighbors
- People in your hobbies
- LinkedIn connections
- Instagram/Twitter mutuals

**Target: 50 names minimum**

### Step 2: The Sort
For each person, ask:
1. Could they hire me?
2. Do they know someone who could?
3. Are they influential in a industry?

**Tag each person: A (hot) / B (warm) / C (long shot)**

### Step 3: The Reach Out

**Template for A-list (Direct potential clients):**
```
Hey [Name],

Quick question - I've started offering [service] to [target industry]. 
Basically I help [specific outcome].

Would this be useful for you guys, or do you know anyone 
who might need this?

No pressure either way - just starting to put the word out.

[Your name]
```

**Template for B-list (Connectors):**
```
Hey [Name]!

I'm launching my freelance [service] business and trying to 
find my first few clients. 

I help [target] with [specific outcome].

If anyone comes to mind who might need this, I'd really 
appreciate an intro. Happy to return the favor however I can.

[Your name]
```

### Step 4: The Follow-Up

Most people need multiple touches:
- Day 0: Initial message
- Day 3: Bump if no response
- Day 7: Different angle or just checking in
- Day 14: Final "no worries if not" message

**Don't be annoying. Don't be invisible.**

## Expected Results
- 50 reach-outs
- 30 responses
- 5-10 leads
- 1-2 clients

# STRATEGY 2: COLD OUTREACH THAT WORKS

## Why Most Cold Outreach Fails
- Generic mass emails
- Talking about yourself
- No clear value prop
- Asking for too much upfront

## The PSA Framework
**P**roblem → **S**olution → **A**sk

### Email Structure:

**Subject Line Examples:**
- Quick question about [their website/content]
- Idea for [Company Name]
- [Specific thing] caught my eye

**Body:**
```
Hi [Name],

[Personal observation about their business - shows you did research]

I noticed [specific problem or opportunity you can help with].

I help [similar companies] with [specific outcome]. For example, 
[brief case study or result].

Would you be open to a quick chat about whether this could 
work for [Company]?

Either way, [genuine compliment about their work].

[Your name]
[One line about you]
```

**Example - for a web designer:**
```
Hi Sarah,

Love what you're doing with EcoWear - the sustainable fashion 
angle is really resonating right now.

I noticed your product pages load in about 6 seconds and the 
mobile checkout flow has some friction points. These typically 
cost e-commerce brands 20-30% in lost conversions.

I help sustainable fashion brands optimize their Shopify stores 
for mobile. Just finished a project for [similar brand] that 
increased their conversion rate by 22%.

Worth a quick chat to see if I could help EcoWear?

Either way, keep crushing it with the brand - the messaging 
is really on point.

Best,
Alex
```

## Profile Optimization Checklist

### Headline (Most Important!)
Bad: "Freelance Designer"
Good: "I design landing pages that convert visitors into customers"

### Profile Photo
- Professional but approachable
- Clear face, good lighting
- Matches your industry vibe

### Overview/Bio Structure
1. Who you help (target client)
2. What you do (specific service)
3. Result you deliver (outcome)
4. Why you (credibility/experience)
5. Call to action

### Portfolio
- 3-5 best pieces only
- Before/after when possible
- Results/context for each
- Most relevant to target niche

## The First 5 Reviews Strategy

Getting reviews when new is hard. Here's how:

1. **Discounted first projects** (50% off first 5)
2. **Portfolio projects** (do work, ask for review)
3. **Free micro-projects** (quick win, review in exchange)

# STRATEGY 4: THE INBOUND SEED PLANTING

## This Won't Get Client #1-5, But Sets Up #6-50

### Content Seeding
While doing outreach, also:
- Post 3x/week on LinkedIn or Twitter
- Share insights, not pitches
- Comment on target industry content
- Help people in communities

### Types of Posts That Work
1. **Process posts** - How you do what you do
2. **Results posts** - Case studies, before/after
3. **Lesson posts** - What you learned from a project
4. **Opinion posts** - Your take on industry trends
5. **Resource posts** - Tools/tips you use

### Minimum Viable Presence
- 3 posts/week
- 10 comments/day on others' content
- 1 value post in a community/week

# DISCOVERY CALL SCRIPT

## Opening (2 min)
"Thanks for taking the time. Quick agenda: I want to learn about what you're working on and see if I can help. If there's a fit, great - we'll discuss next steps. If not, no worries at all. Sound good?"

## Discovery Questions (15 min)
1. "Tell me about [company/project]"
2. "What are you trying to achieve with [area you help with]?"
3. "What's currently working? What's not?"
4. "What have you tried before?"
5. "What would success look like?"
6. "What's the timeline? Budget range?"

## Positioning (5 min)
"Based on what you've shared, here's how I typically help clients in your situation..."

## Next Steps (3 min)
"I'll send over a proposal by [date] with a few options. You can review, ask questions, and we'll go from there."

# PRICING YOUR FIRST PROJECTS

## The New Freelancer Paradox
- You need experience to charge high
- You need to charge to get experience

## The "First 5" Pricing Strategy

| Client # | Discount | Purpose |
|----------|----------|---------|
| 1-2 | 50% off | Get experience + reviews |
| 3-4 | 25% off | Build confidence + case studies |
| 5 | Full price | Prove you can |

## Price Anchoring
Even at discount, show the "real" price:
"This would normally be $500, but for our first project together, I'm offering it at $250."

# COMMON OBJECTIONS & RESPONSES

**"You don't have much experience"**
→ "You're right, I'm newer to freelancing. But I have [X years experience] in [skill]. And because I'm building my portfolio, you get premium work at a fraction of what agencies charge."

**"Can you do it cheaper?"**
→ "I understand budget is important. Let's talk about scope - what's the most important outcome? We might be able to adjust deliverables to fit your budget."

**"We're not ready yet"**
→ "Totally understand. When do you think you would be? I'll follow up then. In the meantime, I'll send over a couple of resources that might help."

**"We're talking to other freelancers"**
→ "Makes sense. What's most important to you in making this decision? I want to make sure I address that in my proposal."
